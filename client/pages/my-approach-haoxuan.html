Chapter 2 — What is calisthenics?

Calisthenics uses your bodyweight and gravity as resistance to build strength, muscle, endurance, flexibility, and body control.

Accessible training

Bodyweight training can require little or no equipment. Your own mass provides resistance while you improve strength, mobility, and cardiovascular endurance.

Calisthenics vs. weightlifting

Calisthenics emphasizes relative strength and whole-body control. Weightlifting uses external loads and can target specific muscles more directly.

Myth: it is only for skinny people

False. Anyone can start, regardless of body type.

Myth: you must already be strong

False. Regressions and progressions allow complete beginners to improve safely.

Myth: it cannot build muscle

False. Progressive bodyweight training can build significant muscle alongside proper nutrition and consistent training.

Chapter 3 — Beginner mindset 🌱

Realistic expectations

Advanced skills take time. Your pace depends on experience, consistency, recovery, and genetics. Aim to become a little better every week—not perfect overnight.

Consistency over perfection

Motivation comes and goes; consistency creates results. Showing up and completing the right session moves you forward.

Trust the process

Fast weeks and slow weeks are both normal. Keep following the program, recover properly, and let small daily improvements add up.

Build discipline

Discipline is doing what needs to be done when you do not feel like it. Create a routine and turn training into a habit.

Avoid comparison

Do not compare your beginning with someone else's years of training. Compare yourself with last month and celebrate every improvement.

Chapter 4 — Goal setting 🎯

Choose a realistic goal

A planche in four weeks from absolute zero is not realistic. Five to ten clean pull-ups with consistent training is a meaningful beginner goal.

Short-term vs. long-term

Short-term goals establish your baseline and foundation. Skills such as handstand push-ups and the planche are long-term projects that can take years.

Track progress

Record your physique, skill performance, reps, holds, and form. Without tracking, it is difficult to see whether you are moving forward.

Build good habits

One deep, controlled push-up beats ten fast, shallow reps. Master push-ups, dips, pull-ups, and other foundations before chasing flashy skills.

Chapter 5 — Equipment guide

You can build an excellent foundation with only the floor and a wall. Push-ups, squats, and wall handstand holds are enough to begin.

Beginner essentials

If you want equipment, start with a pull-up bar and dip bars. Parallettes are useful for L-sits, V-sits, handstands, deeper push-ups, and reducing wrist extension.

Resistance bands

Bands can assist your first pull-up, support progressions, warm up joints, or add resistance.

Chapter 6 — Understanding strength

Progressive overload

Increase the difficulty of your exercises over time. Without a new challenge—more quality reps, range, control, or a harder variation—progress will stall.

Why basics matter

Push-ups, pull-ups, dips, and squats build the strength and coordination needed for skills. A muscle-up, for example, needs sound technique and roughly 10–15 solid pull-ups.

Training frequency

Frequency is how often you train a muscle group or movement pattern. As a general framework, train each muscle group around twice weekly while managing recovery.

Rest days

Training adds stress; downtime is when muscles repair, grow, and restore energy. Seven hard days every week is not the shortcut it seems.

Volume and intensity

Volume is total work (sets × reps × load). Intensity is how difficult the work is relative to your maximum ability or proximity to failure.

Patience and progress

Set milestones, track small wins, and stay resilient through plateaus. Steady progress beats rushing.

Chapter 7 — Recovery (important)

Why recovery matters

Bodyweight training stresses muscles, connective tissue, and your nervous system. Rest supports repair, tendon adaptation, strength gains, and injury prevention.

Sleep and performance

Aim for 7–9 hours each night. Sleep supports motor learning, muscle repair, energy, and the coordination demanded by planches, levers, and muscle-ups.

Muscle recovery

Balance high-intensity skill work with targeted rest, sufficient sleep, and appropriate protein and energy intake.

Deload weeks

Every 4–6 weeks, consider 6–7 easier days with roughly half your usual volume and easier variations to reduce accumulated fatigue.

Stretching and mobility

Before training, use 5–10 minutes of dynamic movement such as arm circles, leg swings, and thoracic twists. Mobility combines usable range with active control.

Warm up

Spend 10–15 minutes moving from light general cardio into joint preparation and specific muscle activation.

Cool down

Use 5–10 minutes to lower your heart rate gradually and gently lengthen the muscles you trained.

Prevent overtraining

Program 2–3 rest days, avoid taking every set to failure, and prioritize sleep, protein, carbohydrates, and sustainable skill frequency.

Listen to your body

Do not ignore sharp shoulder or joint pain. Missing one session is better than losing months to an injury. Stop and seek appropriate professional advice when pain persists.

The 6-week Beginner Program — Start here

Progress takes time. Every exercise and progression has a purpose, so trust the process and avoid skipping ahead. Everyone starts at a different level; improve from where you are today instead of comparing yourself with others.

Complete a strength assessment before week one and record your results. Train with good technique, recover well, and measure again after the program. Quality always beats quantity.

Your graduation targets

  • 15–20 push-ups comfortably.
  • Your first pull-up from zero, or 3–5 pull-ups if you already had one.
  • A 45–60 second plank.
  • A 30–45 second dead hang.
  • 20 bodyweight squats with solid form.
  • A 10–20 second hollow-body hold.

These are motivating targets, not guarantees. Starting ability and progress differ. Showing up, using clean technique, and becoming stronger each week are the real goals.

Monday / Friday — Full Body A

Warm-up (5–10 min): jumping jacks 60 sec; high knees or butt kicks 60 sec; wrist circles 60 sec; arm circles 60 sec; 5 scapular push-ups; 5 scapular pull-ups.

Wall push-ups

3 sets × 4–8 reps · 60 sec rest

Set your body on a diagonal, use full range, and avoid shallow reps.

Dead hangs

2 sets × 10–30 sec · 60 sec rest

Lightly engage your lower traps and lats.

Squats

2–3 sets × 8–12 reps · 30–60 sec rest

Use your safe full range rather than stopping every rep at 90 degrees.

Dead bug

2 sets × 4–6 slow reps per side · 30–60 sec rest

Extend for about 2 seconds, keep your back from arching, and breathe.

Wednesday — Full Body B

Warm-up: repeat Monday's sequence.

Incline push-ups

3 sets × 2–6 reps · 60 sec rest

Keep a straight body and use full range; regress to scapular push-ups if needed.

Active hang

2 sets × 15–30 sec · 45 sec rest

Keep shoulders away from ears, elbows straight, ribs controlled, and breathe.

Reverse lunge

2 sets × 6–8 reps per side · 30–60 sec rest

Keep feet hip-width, front foot flat, hips square, and push through the front leg.

Plank

2 sets × 30–60 sec · 30–45 sec rest

Brace your core and keep your back neutral without sagging or arching.

Cooldown and recovery days

After lifting (5–10 min): chest stretch; lat stretch for 20–30 sec; gentle hamstring stretch.

Tuesday: light walk or bike plus mobility. Thursday: light mobility and rest. Saturday: 20–30 minutes of walking, jogging, basketball, or cycling plus mobility. Sunday: rest, with optional easy movement and stretching.

Phase complete

  • Better technique.
  • More confidence and consistency.
  • Improved strength from your starting point.

Knee push-ups

Keep elbows near 45 degrees, core tight, and body straight. Return to last week's variation if needed.

2–3 sets × 4–8 reps · 45 sec rest

Move through the shoulder blades with straight elbows; lower reps to preserve quality.

Walking lunge

2 sets × 6–8 reps per side · 30–60 sec rest

Use a long controlled step, keep your chest tall, hips square, and drive through the front foot.

Side plank

2 sets × 15–30 sec per side · 45–60 sec rest

Stack your elbow under your shoulder and keep hips lifted without rolling forward.

Wednesday — Full Body B

Warm-up: use the Phase 1 warm-up.

Push-ups

2 sets × 3–6 reps · 60 sec rest

Use full range with a straight body; choose an easier push-up variation if needed.

Australian pull-ups (dip bar required)

2 sets × 3–6 reps · 45 sec rest

Keep hips up, initiate with your back, and do not swing.

Bulgarian split squat

2 sets × 4–8 reps per side · 45–60 sec rest

Use a sensible bench height, keep the front knee tracking well, and control each rep.

Hollow-body hold

2 sets × 15–30 sec · 30–45 sec rest

Brace your abs, keep your lower back connected to the floor, and scale arm or leg position as needed.

Cooldown and recovery

Repeat the Phase 1 cooldown and recovery-day structure. Friday is about refining technique, not forcing difficulty.

Phase complete

  • Cleaner technique and more confidence.
  • Better consistency—good job.
  • Noticeably improved strength.

Monday / Friday — Full Body A

Warm-up: use the Phase 1 warm-up.

Diamond push-ups

2 sets × 6 reps · 60 sec rest

Use an easier close-grip variation if needed. Avoid flared elbows, sagging hips, and partial range.

Band-assisted pull-ups

2–3 sets × 4–8 reps · 45 sec rest

Use enough assistance for full, controlled reps. Reduce reps before sacrificing quality.

Jump squats

2 sets × 6–8 reps · 30–60 sec rest

Squat under control, jump powerfully, and land softly with knees tracking over toes.

Hanging knee raises

2 sets × 5–8 reps · 45–60 sec rest

Control the ascent and descent without swinging or relying on momentum.

Wednesday — Full Body B

Warm-up: use the Phase 1 warm-up.

Decline push-ups

2 sets × 3–6 reps · 60 sec rest

Keep your body straight and use clean full-range reps; regress if this standard is not ready.

Pull-up

2 sets × 2–4 reps · 45 sec rest

If you have earned it, celebrate your first pull-up. Avoid kipping, reach full elbow extension, and engage your shoulder blades. Continue assisted reps if needed.

Assisted pistol squat

2 sets × 3–6 reps per side · 45–60 sec rest

Use a stable support, keep your heel down and knee tracking well, and do not let your arms do all the work.

Hanging leg raises

2 sets × 3–6 reps · 30–45 sec rest

Use only the range you can control without momentum; regress to knee raises when needed.

Cooldown and recovery

Repeat the Phase 1 cooldown and recovery-day structure. Keep Friday technical; Saturday light; Sunday restorative.
